# Chapter VI-A Deductions and Section 24(b) (Old Tax Regime)

**Source Organization:** Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), Ministry of Finance, India  
**Applicable Assessment Years:** AY 2024-25, AY 2025-26, AY 2026-27  
**Document ID:** GOI-CBDT-CH-VIA-DEDUCTIONS-2024  
**Source URL:** https://incometaxindia.gov.in/pages/rules/income-tax-rules-1962.aspx  

---

### 1. Section 80C: Deductions for Investments and Savings
- **Ceiling Limit:** Aggregate deduction under Section 80C, 80CCC, and 80CCD(1) is capped at **₹1,50,000** per financial year.
- **Eligible Instruments:**
  1. Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) and Public Provident Fund (PPF).
  2. Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) mutual funds (with 3-year mandatory lock-in).
  3. Life Insurance Corporation (LIC) and private life insurance premiums.
  4. Principal repayment of housing loan borrowed from approved financial institutions.
  5. Tuition fees for up to two children in Indian universities/schools.
  6. National Savings Certificate (NSC), Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY), Tax-saving 5-year Fixed Deposits.

---

### 2. Section 80D: Deduction for Health Insurance Premiums
Section 80D provides deductions for premiums paid towards health insurance policies and preventive health check-ups:
- **Self, Spouse, and Dependent Children:**
  - Up to **₹25,000** for individuals under 60 years.
  - Up to **₹50,000** if the taxpayer or spouse is a senior citizen (60 years or above).
- **Parents:**
  - Additional deduction of up to **₹25,000** if parents are below 60 years.
  - Additional deduction of up to **₹50,000** if parents are senior citizens (60 years or older).
- **Preventive Health Check-up:**
  - Up to **₹5,000** within the overall limit of ₹25,000/₹50,000.
- **Maximum Combined Deduction:** Up to **₹1,00,000** (when both taxpayer and parents are senior citizens).

---

### 3. Section 80CCD(1B): Additional Deduction for National Pension Scheme (NPS)
- Individual taxpayers can claim an exclusive deduction of up to **₹50,000** for voluntary contributions to the Tier-I National Pension System (NPS).
- This ₹50,000 deduction is over and above the ₹1,50,000 ceiling prescribed under Section 80C, giving a combined deduction of up to **₹2,00,000**.

---

### 4. Section 24(b): Deduction for Interest on Home Loan
- **Self-Occupied Property:** Maximum deduction allowed for interest on borrowed capital is **₹2,00,000** per financial year.
- **Let-Out Property:** Full interest paid can be deducted against rental income, subject to the overall house property loss set-off restriction of **₹2,00,000** against other heads of income in the same financial year under Section 71(3A).
- *Crucial note:* Section 24(b) deduction for self-occupied property is NOT permissible under the New Tax Regime.

---

### 5. Section 80TTA and 80TTB: Interest on Deposits
- **Section 80TTA:** Resident individuals below 60 years can claim deduction up to **₹10,000** on interest earned from savings bank accounts.
- **Section 80TTB:** Senior citizens can claim deduction up to **₹50,000** on interest earned from savings bank accounts, fixed deposits (FD), and recurring deposits (RD).

---

### 6. Old Tax Regime Slabs, Senior Citizen Exemption, and Health & Education Cess
- **Basic Exemption Thresholds under Old Regime:**
  - Individual taxpayers below 60 years: Up to **₹2,50,000** is exempt from income tax (Nil rate).
  - **Senior Citizens (aged 60 to 79 years):** The basic tax slab exemption limit is **₹3,00,000** (Nil tax up to ₹3,00,000).
  - **Super Senior Citizens (aged 80 years and above):** Exemption limit is **₹5,00,000**.
- **Tax Slabs (General Individual):**
  - Up to ₹2,50,000: Nil.
  - ₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000: 5% (eligible for Section 87A rebate of up to ₹12,500).
  - ₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000: 20%.
  - Above ₹10,00,000: 30%.
- **Health and Education Cess:**
  - A mandatory Health and Education Cess at the rate of **4%** is levied on the total amount of income tax plus applicable surcharge across all categories of taxpayers in India under both Old and New Tax Regimes.
